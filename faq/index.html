Working with Us

Most of our engagements start at $50,000 and run for at least 3 months. We find that this is the minimum investment required to deeply understand a business problem, design a robust solution, and deliver measurable results. However, we do occasionally take on smaller strategic audits or technical due diligence projects starting at $15,000.
We work with both. About 60% of our clients are mid-market to enterprise organizations dealing with digital transformation or legacy modernization. The other 40% are well-funded startups (Series A and above) that need to scale their architecture rapidly or bring a complex product to market.
You will work directly with our full-time, in-house team. We do not offshore work to anonymous third parties or bait-and-switch with junior staff after the sales process. The senior strategists and engineers you meet during the discovery phase are the same people who will lead your project.

Technical Approach

We are pragmatic rather than dogmatic. While we have deep expertise in modern ecosystems (React/Vue for frontend, Node/Python/Go for backend, AWS/Azure/GCP for cloud), we choose the right tool for your specific problem. If a problem is best solved by a boring, 10-year-old technology that is stable and easy for your team to maintain, that's what we'll recommend.
Yes. Upon payment, you own 100% of the code, designs, architectures, and intellectual property we create for you. We don't believe in vendor lock-in, and we build systems designed to be eventually handed over to your internal teams.

Process & Pricing

We prefer fixed-price or value-based pricing for well-defined projects because it aligns our incentives with yours: delivering the right outcome efficiently. For ongoing, fluid work (like team augmentation or long-term product iteration), we work on a retained capacity model (reserving a specific block of our team's time per month).
Scope changes are a natural part of software development. When new requirements arise, we discuss the trade-offs immediately. If the new feature is critical, we'll either swap it out for a lower-priority feature currently in scope (keeping the budget/timeline intact) or re-estimate the project with the new scope added.
